The Glow Playbook: six campaigns, five calendars, zero discounts.

Every campaign below exists because a specific finding in the data says the money is there. Each one names its target audience, its promotion, its creative (carousel, static, story, reel — assets rendered and included), the exact scripts, and the single number in Bask that tells you whether it worked. Mock salon: Sunset Ridge Tanning (@sunsetridgetan).

Three rules carried from the data into every promo: ① Never discount the thing being saved — price doesn't drive renewal; presence does. Give upgrades and bundles (beds are 90%+ idle, so an upgrade costs ~nothing). ② Every campaign fires from a list the till can print — day-5 first-timers, day-15 quiet members, expiry+7, 90-days-quiet, broken pairs. ③ Consumer copy never sounds like a spreadsheet — the numbers live here, not in captions.

Campaign 1 · always-on automation

Glow-Back — the day-5 message

Why: three of four returning first-timers are back within 7 days; after a week, odds fall to 3-in-10. ~320 undecided first-timers per salon-year; a rescued one spends ≈ $113 in year one. The follow-up must land on day 5, not month-end.

Audience: first-timers, no 2nd visit by day 5Channels: SMS/DM + IG story + static retargetPromo: free bed upgrade on session 2 (zero marginal cost)

Creative — rendered

SMS / DM script (day 5, from the salon's own number)

"Hey [name] — it's [staffer] from Sunset Ridge ☀️ That first glow suited you. Come back any day this week and I'll bump you to the good bed, no charge. Want me to hold tomorrow at [their first-visit hour]?" — send at the hour of their first visit; it's the hour they already chose once.

Targeting

  • Primary: till-printed day-5 list (name + number). This is the campaign.
  • Support: IG/FB custom audience "new clients last 14 days" — story + static as air cover, $2–3/day.
  • Sep–Nov: double the support budget — autumn first-timers vanish 12 points more and are worth the same.

Caption (static)

That first glow looked good on you. Round two gets the good bed — on us, this week. ☀️ Tap to book. #glowback #sunsetridge #tanningsalon

Measure: second-visit-within-14-days rate on the Bask new-customer card. Baseline 64.2% within 180d; the automation aims at the day-7 pool. Even +1pt ≈ $360/salon-yr at zero media cost.
Campaign 2 · monthly winback flight

The Comeback — "We saved your spot"

Why: the comeback visit is the best sales moment in the shop — membership closes 4.4× and lotion attaches 2.4× the normal rate. A returned lapser typically out-earns a new walk-in. And leavers give no warning, so the 90-days-quiet list is the only way to find them.

Audience: 90-days-quiet customers (list upload, monthly)Channels: DM/SMS + carousel + reelPromo: Comeback Day = VIP day — free upgrade; rejoin today → bottle rides along

DM / SMS script

"[name]! It's been a minute — zero guilt, life happens ☀️ If you come back this month, your first day back is a VIP day: best bed, on us. And if you restart your membership that day, your first bottle of lotion comes with it. Your spot's still yours — want Thursday?"

Counter play (the part social can't do)

  • Front desk flags comeback visits (Bask shows "first visit in 90+ days"). Staff treat it as a first visit: greet by name, re-tour, make the membership ask — this is the 4.4× moment.
  • Bottle on the counter before they leave — 2.4× attach moment, no pitch needed beyond "want your bottle with that?"
Measure: monthly reactivations, comeback-day membership closes (baseline 28.7% — staff scripting aims at 35%+), comeback-day attach (baseline 13.7%).
Campaign 3 · always-on member automation

Use Your Month — the day-15 nudge and the 14-day streak

Why: members you don't see don't renew (16% vs 52% by usage) — visible by day 15. Price does nothing; presence does everything. After expiry there's a flat 14-day window (renew-again ~53%) before the slide to occasional (33%). Two messages, both visit nudges, no discounts ever.

Audience A: members <4 visits by day 15Audience B: expired members, day 7Channels: SMS + story templatesPromo: none / perk-carryover — never a price cut

Story templates — rendered

SMS scripts

Day 15, quiet member: "Hey [name] — halfway through your month and the bed's been asking about you ☀️ Tonight's wide open. Want 7:30?" — booking link, no offer. The product being sold is the visit.

Expiry + 7: "Your month wrapped last week, [name]. Restart in the next 7 days and everything carries straight over — same bed, same rate, streak intact. One tap: [link]"

Counter play

  • Renewal moment = the quietest register (attach 7.7% vs 19.2% at signup). One scripted line on every renewal: "want the bottle on the package?"
  • If a member hesitates at renewal: offer the cheaper tier, never a discount — downshifters outlast non-changers (6.1 vs 5.2 packages).
Measure: first-cycle renewal (baseline 32.0%), share of renewals landing ≤14 days after expiry, renewal-day attach.
Campaign 4 · flagship flight (spring + fall)

Tan Together — the Duo Month

Why: 20.6% of visits arrive as pairs; stable pairs are 5% of customers holding 12% of revenue; when one partner quits, the survivor's visits drop 45% within 90 days. The pair is the unit of loyalty — so sell to it, and defend it.

Audience: members + their plus-ones; broken pairs (partner quiet 60d+)Channels: reel + carousel + UGC + DMPromo: Duo Month (add your person for half) · Bring-your-person week (friend's first session free)

Campaign 5 · retail flight — UVALUX co-op

Graduate to the Bottle — sachet trade-up month

Why: 7 in 10 lotion buyers start on the sachet at ~$19/oz; only 15% ever reach the bottle ($4.50/oz) — yet half of sachet buyers repurchase. The trade-up moment walks to the register on its own; nobody graduates without being asked. Run in March — the reorder ramp inflects then, and UVALUX's co-op dollars fund the flight.

Audience: in-store buyers + IG followers (education)Channels: carousel + counter card + register scriptPromo: this month's sachets count toward your bottle (30-day window)

Carousel — rendered (5 slides)

Register script (the whole campaign in one sentence)

On every second sachet purchase: "Fun fact — two of these costs more than half the bottle. Want me to put today's toward one?" Bask flags the second-sachet moment at the till.

UVALUX co-op angle (Compass)

  • Sachet→bottle conversion becomes the per-account metric a rep sells against (baseline 15%).
  • UVALUX funds the counter cards + the trade-up credit; the salon supplies the ask. Bottle velocity is the shared win.
  • Timing: March flight = 3 weeks ahead of the 12.6% demand step at week 9–10.
Measure: sachet→bottle conversion (15% → 25% target), bottle units, attach on second-sachet transactions.
Campaign 6 · season flights

Season Flights — January believers, summer crews

Why: January joiners renew at ~39% vs ~20% for July — the New-Year member is nearly twice the member. And pairs of brand-new faces are event tanners (they return less), so summer sells the occasion, not the membership — then the day-5 machine chases each crew member individually.

January: membership pushMay–July: group/event packagesChannels: static + reel + local targetingPromos: January join week · Crew packages (built around a date)

Summer crew play

Occasion packages ("Wedding Crew", "Vacay Crew") sold per-date, not per-month — the data says summer joiners were never going to renew, so package the event honestly and win the goodbye. Every crew member lands on the day-5 list afterward; the few with habit potential surface themselves.

Measure: January joins (each worth ~1.4× an average member, ~1.9× a July one); crew bookings; day-5 conversion of crew members post-event.
