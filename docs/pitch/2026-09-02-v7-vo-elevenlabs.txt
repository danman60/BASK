Bask is an app a salon owner opens when the place is quiet. To make it less quiet.

It reads last night's numbers before anyone unlocks the door. Not a dashboard. A short letter, in plain words, about what actually changed.

Lotion per visit slipped. Not everywhere — Thursday afternoons, and Friday nights. Traffic never moved. That gap is four thousand dollars a month, and it had been there for four weeks.

Then the part nobody else does. It doesn't invent the advice. It goes and finds where that advice came from — the training you've already recorded, a thousand pieces of coaching, and it shows you the words somebody actually said on a stage about this exact problem.

One button. The campaign is already written. The right people, the right night, the offer inside your own rules. You read it, you change what you want, and you send it. Nothing leaves without you.

And you're not doing it alone. Other owners are right there, working the same week.

Then it comes back and tells you what it was worth. Not a promise. A measured number, next to the coaching that suggested it.

Everything on this screen can be opened. Every figure goes back to the visits behind it.

Same nervous system, opposite end. Every salon running this is a signal, and for the first time UVALUX can see the market it actually serves.

Your rep stops calling to ask if anyone needs anything. They call already knowing what's wrong, and what to suggest.

And the coaching underneath all of it is yours. Your training, your sessions, reviewed by your people before it ever reaches a salon floor.

That's Bask. It opens when the salon is quiet. To make it less quiet.
