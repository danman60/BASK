UVALUX Intelligence Platform

Phase 1 — Proof of Concept Proposal

Prepared for: UVALUX International Inc.
Prepared by: Daniel Abrahamson
Date: August 2026

The Opportunity

UVALUX already sits in a uniquely valuable position.

It has deep relationships with salon owners, extensive purchasing and equipment data, industry expertise, coaching knowledge, hosted salon data, and a growing ability to understand how successful salons actually operate.

The opportunity is to connect those assets into a platform that does more than report numbers.

The goal is to create a system that can tell a salon owner:

What is happening in my business, where am I underperforming, what should I do next, and can you help me do it?

At the same time, UVALUX gains a new view across its customer network:

Which businesses are healthy, which need help, where opportunities exist, and what action should a UVALUX representative take next?

Rather than building another salon-management/POS platform, the initial focus is the greenfield opportunity around:

Proposed Three-Phase Approach

The project should be deliberately staged.

Each phase creates a clear decision point before committing additional capital or development effort.

Phase 1

Proof of Concept

Build a working product with a small number of datasets and friendly stakeholders.

The purpose is to establish whether the underlying concept produces useful and actionable business intelligence.

Question Phase 1 answers:

Can we take UVALUX and salon operating data and consistently identify opportunities that help salons operate and grow more effectively?

Phase 2

Canadian Pilot & Commercial Product

If Phase 1 proves compelling, expand the product into a polished pilot suitable for real UVALUX customers.

This phase would introduce additional data sources, customer testing, automation, benchmarking and a production-oriented salon experience.

The goal would be to have a credible product UVALUX can demonstrate to Canadian customers and at industry events.

Phase 2 would be separately scoped and approved after Phase 1.

Phase 3

Commercial Scale

Once product-market fit is demonstrated in Canada, develop the commercial model for broader deployment.

Potential expansion includes:

  • Canadian-wide rollout
  • U.S. salon market
  • UVALUX-hosted software customers
  • partnerships and integrations
  • wellness and recovery businesses
  • broader vertical opportunities

At this stage, the parties can determine the appropriate long-term structure, including commercial licensing, recurring platform economics, ownership participation, equity or another partnership structure.

Phase 1 Objective

Build a functional intelligence platform that demonstrates the complete loop:

text BUSINESS DATA ↓ ANALYTICS ↓ OPPORTUNITY DETECTION ↓ INDUSTRY / UVALUX KNOWLEDGE ↓ AI INTERPRETATION ↓ ACTIONABLE RECOMMENDATION ↓ BUSINESS ACTION ↓ MEASURED RESULT

The product should be demonstrable with real or representative salon data, not merely a static prototype.

2. UVALUX Intelligence

A UVALUX-facing view of the customer network.

The system should help answer:

  • Which accounts are growing?
  • Which accounts are declining?
  • Which salons need coaching?
  • Which accounts have unusual purchasing patterns?
  • Which customers may be underbuying profitable categories?
  • Which customers may need inventory?
  • Who may be ready for additional equipment?
  • Where are there membership or retention opportunities?

  • What should they discuss?

The objective is to turn account management from:

into:

Here are the five accounts where UVALUX can create the most value today.

Benchmarking

A key component of the proof of concept will be demonstrating how participating salons can understand their performance relative to comparable businesses.

Potential measures include:

  • revenue per customer
  • unique customers
  • visits
  • membership penetration
  • membership retention
  • average membership duration
  • retail attachment
  • product sales
  • customer frequency
  • customer retention
  • equipment utilization
  • average transaction value
  • recurring revenue
  • service mix

The intention is not to expose individual businesses to one another.

Instead, data can be anonymized and aggregated into useful benchmarking such as:

Your retail attachment is in the 72nd percentile among comparable salons.

or:

Your average membership lasts 3.1 months, compared with 2.6 months across comparable businesses.

UVALUX Knowledge Layer

The system should also begin capturing the expertise that already exists inside UVALUX.

Potential sources include:

  • coaching sessions
  • training videos
  • seminar transcripts
  • product education
  • sales training
  • membership guidance
  • business-planning material
  • internal experts
  • successful salon case studies
  • recorded presentations

The platform can retrieve relevant UVALUX expertise when a business problem is identified.

For example:

text DATA SIGNAL Retail attachment declining ↓ UVALUX KNOWLEDGE Relevant retail coaching ↓ AI Combines current business facts with UVALUX expertise ↓ OWNER Specific recommended action

The goal is not to replace UVALUX's human experts.

It is to make their expertise available more consistently and allow the human team to focus on higher-value conversations.

Action Layer

Phase 1 should demonstrate that intelligence does not have to stop at a dashboard.

Recommendations can lead directly to useful actions such as:

  • customer lists
  • staff scripts
  • membership conversations
  • SMS campaigns
  • email campaigns
  • social posts
  • coaching plans
  • promotions
  • inventory recommendations
  • equipment opportunities
  • UVALUX rep tasks

The product principle is:

Every useful insight should point toward something someone can actually do.

This may include:

Salon data

  • equipment usage

Existing hosted data

Where UVALUX already has appropriate access and customer authorization.

File/database ingestion

For selected pilot salons, historical exports or databases may be imported directly.

The architecture will be designed so additional integrations can be introduced later without rebuilding the intelligence layer.

Privacy and Data Ownership

Phase 1 should establish the appropriate technical and commercial model before broad customer deployment.

Key principles:

  • salon data remains protected
  • customer-level data is not marketed independently by UVALUX
  • benchmarking is anonymized where appropriate
  • salons explicitly authorize use of their operational data
  • data is used to provide value back to the salon
  • access is controlled by tenant/business
  • sensitive data is minimized
  • appropriate Canadian data residency can be supported where required

Production privacy, consent and legal requirements will be formalized before broad rollout.

Phase 1 Success Criteria

Phase 1 is successful if the parties can demonstrate:

1. Data can be collected and normalized

Different data sources can be converted into a usable common structure.

2. Useful business conditions can be detected

The platform finds opportunities that a knowledgeable operator considers legitimate.

3. Recommendations are actionable

The platform explains what happened, why it matters and what someone should do.

4. UVALUX expertise improves the recommendation

The product incorporates industry-specific knowledge rather than providing generic AI advice.

5. Business value can be measured

Where actions are taken during the pilot, the platform can begin measuring the outcome.

6. Customers want it

Selected salon owners find the information valuable enough to continue using or testing the product.

Timing

Phase 1 is intended to be a focused MVP/proof-of-concept build rather than a production rollout.

The objective is to have the core product operational and testable during Q3, creating enough evidence to make an informed Phase 2 decision.

Phase 1 is a twelve-week engagement from kickoff.

Those twelve weeks begin when UVALUX provides the sample data export and names the pilot stakeholders. Both are required before the build can start, so the schedule does not run while waiting for them.

Phase 1 delivers the items listed under Phase 1 Deliverables. Work beyond that list is Phase 2 scope, agreed separately.

Development milestones within the twelve weeks will be established at kickoff.

Why Start Small

The opportunity could become large.

That is precisely why the first commitment should remain small and measurable.

Rather than attempting to define an enormous software platform in advance:

Build something useful.

Put it in front of real businesses.

Measure whether it creates value.

Approve the next investment based on evidence.

This creates controlled risk for UVALUX while allowing both parties to discover what the market actually values.

The Long-Term Vision

The broader opportunity is to create an intelligence layer sitting between business data and business action.

For the salon owner:

Tell me how my business is doing and what I should do next.

For UVALUX:

Tell me which customer needs help, why, and how we can help them.

For the network:

Learn what actually works across thousands of business decisions and use that knowledge to make every participating business smarter.

The first step is simply proving that loop works.
