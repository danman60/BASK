It is a beer. He talks first and longer. Nothing needs to happen today, and if he starts reaching for a decision, take the pressure off him yourself.
Getting into it easy

Catch up properly first. Business comes up on its own, it always does.

"How is the season treating you? What is actually keeping you busy right now?"

When it turns to what you have been building, keep it small:

"I have been building something for salons. I would rather know what you think of it than pitch you on it, so tell me where it is wrong."

Then stop talking. Whatever he says next is the meeting.

Things worth learning let them come up

Not a list to work through. These are what you want to walk away knowing, in whatever order they arrive.

  • What salons are running now, and what complaints keep reaching him.
  • What a rep wishes they knew before they walk into a salon.
  • Where the privacy line is that neither UVALUX nor an owner would cross.
  • Whether new openings or existing salons are the easier door.
  • Who else at UVALUX would have to like this for it to be real.

Best follow up you have, and it costs nothing: "What else?" Keep asking until he runs dry.

Straight answers if he pokes

He will find the edges. Getting there first is the whole credibility play.

Is this real software?

Not yet. It is a working build to test the idea. The production work is still ahead.

Does it run T-Max or the beds?

Not today. Designed for it, but I am not promising hardware I have not bench tested.

Are the payments and messages real?

No. Simulated in this build. Real providers are still a decision.

Is there an app?

No native app. What you are holding is the web version on a phone.

Who owns the data?

The salon does, and it controls what gets shared. Compass sees business signals, never customer names, contacts or transactions. Legal still has to bless that.

What about moving salons off what they use now?

New salons first, help the rest later. Not promising a migration path before we look at the systems.

If you do not know: "No idea yet. Let me check and I will tell you by Friday." Then write it down in front of him.

If money comes up it should not, today

"We have not priced it. I would rather find out whether owners actually want it before I pretend I know what it costs."

That is the whole answer. Do not fill the silence after it.

Only if he pushes for a number, and label it as thinking out loud, not a quote: roughly CAD $199 a location for the core, $299 with the intelligence layer, and 2 to 3 salons as a first group. None of it approved, none of it a quote.

If he opens structure himself, let him. Reseller, revenue share, licence and investment are all live. Ask which one matches how UVALUX actually works.

Why UVALUX would care only if he asks it himself

Do not raise this. It is what you reach for if he asks why a distributor should build software instead of selling more product.

"The software is not the business. It makes the distribution business worth more."

The part that is not subscription revenue

Salons that run their day on it do not drift. Reps know the account before they walk in. Forecasting comes off real shelf movement instead of a phone call. Reorders write themselves. You can see which salon needs coaching and which one is ready to expand. That lands on the customer relationship UVALUX already owns, not on a separate software account.

The category already pays for worse

Roughly US$100 to $200 a location a month for core salon software, before payments, texting, marketing, kiosks or analytics. TanTrack claims 5,000-plus salons and one in three — their claim, directional only. Helios has been at it since 1988 and still will not post a price.

If he wants a size, build it in front of him

Bottom-up only, never a headline market report. 10,000 locations at US$125 is US$15M a year. 20,000 at US$175 is US$42M. 30,000 at US$250 is US$90M. Then say the next sentence out loud: these are scenarios, not a claim that those salons exist.

If it turns into a real conversation, this is the question to hand him:

"Can UVALUX put a high-retention software product into a network it already serves, make recurring revenue off it, strengthen the distribution business underneath, and move into wellness later?"

That can be a good answer even if tanning software on its own is a modest category. Say that part too. The TAM is the weakest leg of this case and overselling it is how you lose him.

Anchor for the $199 / $299 above, if it is still live: it replaces salon software plus a marketing subscription plus a fraction of an agency.

Three things to plant not asks

Say each one once, as curiosity. Then leave it alone. Pushing any of these twice is what turns a beer into a pitch.
