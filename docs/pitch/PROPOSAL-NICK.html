What I'm proposing to build

A benchmarking, insight and coaching layer for salons, fed by data you already hold.

Not a POS. Not booking. Not a room board. Those come out.

  1. The scoreboard. A salon's numbers come back as a rank — percentile against the group, by category, month over month. Participants see themselves; non-participants see the anonymized industry line. The thing owners in California told you they'd love. Units as well as dollars, because "you sell 35 tanning lotion while the average is 22" is the version that lands.
  2. Customer health. Every customer scored on one grid — loyal, slipping, gone. Lapsed ones come with a reach-out ready to send. At the desk, whoever is serving someone sees that it's been 85 days and what that person usually buys.
  3. Bottle depletion. You don't track how full the bottle is, but you track tans used, last purchase, and roughly half an ounce per tan — so it's computable. The reorder conversation happens before the customer thinks of it, and it cues the sister product when they've plateaued.
  4. Campaigns off the data. One click from "Thursdays are soft" to the offer, the post and the text — tuned to this industry and driven by that salon's own numbers, which is what stops it looking like everyone else's ChatGPT output. This replaces what HootSuite is doing for you now.
  5. Coaching, systematized — delivered by you. Elaine's coaching, Mike's numbers, Sarah's sessions become a knowledge base the product reasons over, and it surfaces what to say. You and Elaine stay the experts and the face. People buy from people; the software only makes sure the right conversation happens more often.

Built to plug into other platforms through an API from day one, and sector-agnostic — so when Angie is knocking on spas, saunas and gyms, there's something to sell her.
