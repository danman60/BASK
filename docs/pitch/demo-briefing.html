Before you start

What is computed, and what is illustrative

Four things sit on the first screen. Two are derived from the salon's visit data and two are content. Know which, out loud, before Wilfred asks.

Daybreak letter — “12% above your usual Wednesday” Computed Derived by loadToday() from real visit rows
“6 ways to grow your business today” Illustrative Fixtures — DEMO_OPPORTUNITIES
Wins feed — what other owners tried Illustrative Fixtures — DEMO_WINS
“What needs your attention” queue Computed Real derived insights. Each one opens to the rows behind it
Say it first, unprompted

“Two things on this page are illustrative and I’ll tell you which as we go — the growth tiles and the community feed are content, not measurement. Everything below them, and everything with a Show me why, is computed off the till.”

Saying it first costs nothing. Being caught on it costs the meeting.

Six clicks · about six minutes

The spine

You do not have to show everything. This is the whole story in order.

  1. 1 Today. Let them read the Daybreak letter. Say nothing while they read. Silence is fine. “Nobody ran a report. The software did the analysis overnight and wrote it in plain English.”
  2. 2 The retail card in the attention queue. Attachment fell 8.3% → 5.9%, most of it evening shifts, about $4,260 a month. “It found this in her own numbers. It also priced it.”
  3. 3 Show me why. The chart, then the actual visits behind it. “Every figure opens. This is the list of visits the percentage was counted from.”
  4. 4 What this drew on. The coaching citations. Open one to the quote and its timecode. “The advice isn’t invented. It’s your training, retrieved, with the words somebody actually said on a stage and where in the session they said it.” Elaine’s beat — slow down here
  5. 5 Fix this → Studio. The campaign arrives pre-filled. Edit one line live. “I never started from a blank page, and nothing sends until she says so.”
  6. 6 Switch to Compass. The theme flips dark. Network, then the Call List. “Same nervous system, opposite end. The rep doesn’t call asking if you need anything. They call knowing what’s wrong and what to suggest.”
Product training

Elaine will ask

Open with this — it is hers

“The worksheet you hand out — sessions divided by unique customers, revenue per session — that is computed in the product. Your definitions are the ones it uses.”

True and checkable: Mike Blore refers to “that sheet Elaine provided” on the expo recording, and revenuePerSession and sessionsPerCustomer are exactly what the metrics module computes. It is the most personal thing you can open with.

How much of our training is in there?

1,007 claims, all embedded and searchable. The review queue header reads 741 because it shows two lenses — advice (469) and recall (272). The third lens, marketing, is the remaining 266. Every claim carries the quote it came from and a timecode into the session.

Who checked these? How many are approved?

Say the real number: 3 of 741 decided. Do not soften it. “Almost none, and that’s deliberate — it’s your queue, not mine. The mining is done and the retrieval works. What’s missing is exactly the judgment you’d own.” The unowned queue is the invitation, and it is the strongest thing you can say to her.

What if a claim is wrong?

Reject it and it stops reaching salons immediately. The retrieval function excludes rejected claims at the database level, so it is a real gate, not a label.

Do the trainers’ names show anywhere?

No. Never rendered, and not in the type — the citation shape has no speaker field at all.

Data

Wilfred will ask

Is this our data?

Separate the two and say so first. The real data is the closed salon group — 194,672 visits, four locations, twelve years — and that is document 1, the breakdown you opened with. The live demo runs on a synthetic salon, Sunset Ridge, whose behaviour was calibrated to that real data (attachment 5.2–9.4%, house about 5.8%). The real salons are closed and generate no live insights, which is exactly why they are a report and not the demo. Do not merge the two. Inside the demo every figure still opens to the rows it was computed from.

Where would our data physically live?

Canadian data in Canada — the same reasoning behind the hosting choice UVALUX already made. Do not offer Netcup as the answer: it is in Manassas, Virginia, US soil. It is the right place to test and the wrong answer to this question. Residency is an open item and saying so is what earns him.

Show me a number reconciling.

Do it rather than answer it. Retail card → Show me why → the records table. The page prints its own working: 84 of 1,441 visits over the last 14 days included a product — 5.8%, then lists them.

What does UVALUX see about a specific salon?

Counts and health bands, never a customer list. One consent filter sits in front of every Compass read and no query routes around it. There is a settings screen for it; it is not in this walkthrough.

Can I move the clock?

Yes, and let him. Presenter Panel is ⌘ ⇧ D. The pipeline recomputes — campaign settles, rollups, insight sweep, brief regenerates. For a data person this is the most convincing thing in the demo, because the numbers move together.

Recovery

If something breaks

  • Any beat fumbles → ⌘ ⇧ D jumps to one of seven bookmarks, so you never navigate under pressure.
  • A surface is slow → talk over it. The Daybreak letter and the citations are the two moments worth waiting for. Narrate past everything else.
  • Citations missing after a generate → the model call failed and the system fell back to templates, which carry no citations by design because they never saw a claim. Say that. It is true, and it is the honest behaviour rather than a bug.
