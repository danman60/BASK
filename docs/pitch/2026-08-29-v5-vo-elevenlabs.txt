This is Bask. It's an app a salon owner opens when the salon is quiet — to make it less quiet.

Every morning it reads last night's takings and says, in plain English, what changed. Then it lists the openings, biggest first. Not a report. Things to do today.

Each one carries the method it came from. Not generic marketing advice — the coaching UVALUX teaches this trade, applied to this salon's numbers.

And it doesn't stop at the advice. It drafts the campaign: the words, the list of who to send it to, the timing. She reads it, changes what she wants, and fires it with one button. Nothing sends until she says so.

Then it counts what came back. Who walked in, what they bought, what the campaign was actually worth.

And she isn't doing any of it alone. Other owners post what they tried and what it did — the same loop, running down the road, with the numbers attached.

None of it is guesswork. It reads the till. A hundred and ninety-four thousand real visits.

There's a second side to it. UVALUX sees the same intelligence one level up: how the salons it serves are actually doing — in counts, never names. So a rep calls knowing what to talk about.

And UVALUX owns the library it all coaches from. Their own training sessions, turned into seven hundred and forty-one claims, reviewed one at a time.

Bask. It opens when the salon is quiet, and it exists to make it less quiet.
